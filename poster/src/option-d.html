Forecasting Under Capacity Mismatch

Problem Statement & Motivation

New PV plants start with no generation history. A model trained on a larger plant learns its output magnitude and overfits to it when transferred to a smaller one.

Magnitude overfitting under a ten-fold capacity gap.

Objectives

  1. Investigate zero-target-label, unsupervised transfer.
  2. Decouple scale through per-unit normalization.
  3. Remove the daily cycle with the Clear-Sky Index (CSI).
  4. Align dynamics with a conditional domain adversarial network (CDAN).

Key Contributions & Data

1

Failure-mode characterization

Shows that magnitude overfitting causes the collapse when source and target capacities differ.

2

Physics-guided domain alignment

Per-unit scaling and the CSI remove capacity and daily-cycle effects before CDAN aligns what remains.

3

Transfer boundary delineation

Tests transfer within India and across continents to Australia, where the seasons are reversed.

Discussion & Future Work

Key insight

Adversarial alignment works well within a region. Across continents (India to Australia) it causes negative transfer (R² < 0), because the seasons are out of phase. An unadapted Transformer is more robust there and keeps positive skill.

Conclusion

  • Per-unit scaling and nocturnal zero-masking fix the magnitude collapse of the uncalibrated baseline.
  • Defines where adaptation helps: within a region, but not across hemispheres.
  • Establishes a zero-target-label transfer benchmark under capacity mismatch.
  • Future work: fine-tuning after commissioning, once the first target labels arrive.
